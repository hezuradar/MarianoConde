Differences between flamenco and classical guitar

Discover the main differences between the flamenco guitar and the classical guitar: construction, materials, sound and techniques. Learn which one best suits your musical style with the experience of the Mariano Conde workshop

Workshop Mariano Conde

In the universe of the Spanish guitar, the distinction between classical and flamenco guitar is fundamental to understand both the evolution of the instrument and its role in different musical genres. Although at first glance they may seem almost identical, their technical and sonic differences are notable and respond to the demands of the styles they represent. From the Mariano Conde workshop, where tradition and innovation go hand in hand in each instrument, we delve into the keys that separate and define these two great pillars of the guitar.

Construction and design: the essence of each style

Soundboard and dimensions

One of the first differences that jumps out at you (and to the touch) is the size and shape of the soundboard.The flamenco guitarIt usually has a narrower and shallower case than the classic one. This translates into a lighter and more manageable instrument, ideal for accompanying singing and dancing, where agility is essential. The rims (the sides of the case) are thinner and the distance between the top and the back is smaller, which contributes to a more immediate and percussive sound response.

On the contrary, the classical guitar opts for a larger and deeper body. This gives it greater volume, resonance and sustain, essential qualities for solo performance and academic repertoire.

Materials: woods that define the character

The type of wood used is another crucial factor.. On the flamenco guitar, Spanish cypress is the traditional choice for the sides and back, while the top is usually German spruce. Cypress is a light and porous wood, which favors a bright sound with a fast attack. Sometimes, especially in models called “black flamenco”, rosewood is used, which provides a deeper nuance that is closer to the classic tone.

The classical guitarOn the other hand, it uses denser woods such as Indian or Brazilian rosewood for the sides and back, and cedar or spruce for the top. These woods provide greater weight and a warmer, rounder sound with greater projection.

Bracing and internal structure

The bracing, that is, the arrangement of the internal bars that reinforce the soundboard, is different in both models. On the flamenco guitar, bracing is designed to offer a quick response, with less sustain and greater percussion, adapting to the strumming and striking techniques characteristic of flamenco. In classical guitar, bracing seeks to maximize the depth, balance and duration of each note, allowing for greater harmonic and dynamic richness.

Action and string height

The action, or height of the strings with respect to the fingerboard, is noticeably lower on the flamenco guitar. This facilitates the execution of fast techniques, slurs and percussive hits, although it implies a slight “cerdeo” or intentional fretting, which provides that characteristic metallic and torn sound. On the classical guitar, the action is higher to avoid any fretting and promote cleanliness and purity of the sound, although it requires greater force in the touch.

Weight and ergonomics

The flamenco guitar, thanks to its light woods and thinner case, is lighter and more comfortable for long sessions, something essential for flamenco guitarists who accompany singing and dancing for hours. The classical guitar, heavier and more robust, is designed for projection in concert halls and the interpretation of complex works.

Sound: the soul of each guitar

Timbre and projection

The sound of the flamenco guitar is bright, sharp and percussive, with a fast attack and a short duration of the notes (little sustain). This quality is essential to stand out between singing and zapateado, providing rhythm and energy to the ensemble. The tapping of the lid (hit) and strumming are an integral part of their expressive language.
For its part, the classical guitar offers a rounder, deeper and more balanced sound, with a prolonged sustain that allows the development of complex melodies and harmonies. Projection and timbral richness are priorities, since the instrument usually performs alone or in small chamber ensembles.

Techniques and execution

The techniques used in each style also influence the construction. Flamenco requires speed, ligados, picados, picks and blows, while classical guitar focuses on clarity, dynamic control and melodic expression. The flamenco neck is usually thinner, facilitating speed and comfort in the left hand.

Additional Differentiating Elements

  • Pickguard: The flamenco guitar usually incorporates a transparent pickguard on the top to protect it from rhythmic hits, something unnecessary on the classical guitar.

Conclusion: which one to choose?

The choice between a flamenco guitar and a classical guitar depends on the musical style, the techniques to be used and the personal preferences of the guitarist. The flamenco guitar is the ideal companion for those looking for a light, agile instrument with a bright and percussive sound, perfect for accompaniment and improvisation. The classical guitar, on the other hand, is the option for those who prioritize melodic richness, projection and sonic depth, being the instrument par excellence for solo performance and academic repertoire.
At Mariano Conde, each guitar is the result of decades of experience and passion for luthiery, adapting every detail to the needs of the musician and the genre. Because understanding the differences between flamenco and classical guitar is, ultimately, understanding the richness and diversity of the Spanish guitar in all its fullness.
