Cutaway on a guitar: What is it and what does it imply?

Discover what a cutaway is on a guitar, its types and advantages for modern players. Improve your technique and style with this essential feature

Workshop Mariano Conde

The termcutawayon a guitar refers to a design feature in the instrument's body: a cutout or notch (usually at the bottom of the body next to the neck) that allows much more comfortable access tothe fretsbottom of thetuning fork. This detail, which may seem minor, has a significant impact on both the playing experience and the aesthetics of the instrument.

How is the cutaway recognized?

The cutaway is easily identified by its “recess” in the body of the guitar, right where the neck joins the body.

Types of cutaway

There are mainly two clipping styles:

  • Venetian cutaway: With soft and rounded lines.
  • Florentine cutaway: With a more angular and pointed contour.

Both fulfill the same function, differing only in aesthetics.

Advantages of the cutaway on a guitar

  • Access to the treble frets: Its main advantage is the ease of reaching the frets following the 12th to the 19th or the 20th fret in this case, which is essential for solos, complex melodies or advanced techniques, especially in styles such as jazz, rock, modern flamenco or fingerpicking.
  • Interpretive versatility: Allows you to explore a wider range of notes and technical resources, opening up expressive possibilities that would be difficult on a guitar without a cutaway.
  • Comfort: It is especially useful for guitarists with small hands or for those who require quick and precise movements in the high register.
  • modern aesthetic: The cutaway design provides a contemporary and elegant image, highly appreciated by many current musicians.

Disadvantages of the cutaway on a guitar

  • Price: Cutaway guitars tend to be a little more expensive, both due to the additional work in construction and the possible inclusion of electronic systems

Who is a cutaway guitar recommended for?

The cutaway is ideal for guitarists who:

  • They play solos or pieces that require access to the upper frets.
  • They perform modern styles, jazz, contemporary flamenco, pop or rock.
  • They are looking for greater comfort and versatility on stage or in recordings.
  • They use the amplified guitar, since many cutaway guitars incorporate electronic systems.

Conclusion

The cutaway on a guitar is an innovation that responds to the technical and stylistic needs of contemporary musicians. Its main contribution is comfort and access to high registers and notes that become more complicated after the 12th fret.
The choice between a guitar with or without a cutaway should be based on each guitarist's playing style, technical needs, and personal preferences.
