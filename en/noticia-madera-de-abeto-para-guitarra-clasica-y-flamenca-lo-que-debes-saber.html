Spruce wood for classical and flamenco guitar. What you should know

German spruce wood, traditional in classical and flamenco guitars, offers bright sound, rich projection and tonal evolution over time.

Workshop Mariano Conde

German spruce pine (Picea abies) is one of the most traditional and appreciated woods for the construction of soundboards on classical and flamenco guitars, standing out for its unique characteristics and exceptional sound properties.

Physical characteristics

Density and color

Fir is a pale yellow wood, almost white, when new, which tends to darken to an amber tone over time. It is a light but rigid wood, with a density between 440-470 kg/m³. It is a light wood with a characteristic ivory shine, with no visible difference between sapwood and heartwood.

Structure

It has clearly visible and marked growth rings, with a very straight fiber and fine grain. Its texture is uniform, which facilitates its workability.

Sound properties

Bright and complex sound

The spruce top provides a bright, harmonic-rich sound, with a wide range of sonic nuances. It offers great projection and dynamics, being especially suitable for musicians looking for a more incisive tone.

Sound evolution

A distinctive feature of spruce is that its sound evolves significantly over time. Of course, it has to have sound and quality from the first moment. If a new guitar does not express its sound correctly, it is not and will not be a good guitar. The theory used by bad guitarists that over time a non-existent sound can emerge, resurrect or appear cannot be applied, no matter how long you wait for it to happen.

Technical aspects

Construction

The most used spruce for classical guitars is Picea abies, known as European spruce or Norway spruce. This wood is especially valued for its excellent relationship between elasticity and weight.

Structural properties

  • High rigidity compared to its weight
  • Great elasticity
  • rectangular cell structure
  • Excellent acoustic response

Practical considerations

Availability

Currently, obtaining it is increasingly expensive due to the scarcity of forests in Germany and Romania.

Maintenance

Fir requires specific care since it is a wood that is sensitive to changes in humidity and temperature. It is important to keep the guitar in stable environmental conditions to preserve its sound qualities.

Ideal applications

Spruce top guitars are particularly suitable for:

  • Repertoire that requires great sound projection
  • Musicians looking for a wide tonal palette and balanced sound
