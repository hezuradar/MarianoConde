Reasons why a guitar goes out of tune

Discover why your guitar goes out of tune and how to maintain stable tuning with good habits, care and maintenance adjustments.

Workshop Mariano Conde

A classical or flamenco guitar can be a faithful life companion, but it can also become a challenge when it is frequently out of tune. Tuning is the foundation of a good sound, and any variations can affect the performance and enjoyment of the instrument.

Why does a guitar go out of tune?The reasons are multiple and, in many cases, avoidable with proper care.

Common causes of detuning

One of the most common causes is changing strings. New strings tend to stretch and settle during the first few days, causing a loss of tension and, therefore, detuning.

It is normal that, after changing the strings, it is necessary to tune the guitar several times until they stabilize. On the other hand, old strings lose elasticity and go out of tune more easily, so it is advisable to change them regularly, depending on use and if you notice that they no longer sound as they should.

Another common cause is the condition of the tuning pegs. If the tuners are loose, damaged, or incorrectly adjusted, the guitar will not be able to maintain tuning for a long time.

The pins should turn smoothly but firmly. If they are too loose, the strings will slip and the guitar will quickly go out of tune. In these cases, it is advisable to adjust them, replace them with higher quality ones or go to a specialized luthier.

Environmental and mechanical factors

Environmental conditions play a fundamental role in guitar tuning. Changes in temperature and humidity can cause wood to expand or contract, altering the tension of the strings.

Wooden guitars are especially sensitive to humidity, so it is advisable to store them in a hard case and, if possible, in an environment with stable conditions.

Mechanical problems, such as a poorly adjusted bridge or worn nut, can also cause out of tune. If the strings do not slide correctly at these points, the tuning will be unstable.

This type of problem usually requires the intervention of a luthier to guarantee an adequate and lasting solution.

Tuning and maintenance technique

The way the guitar is tuned directly influences its stability. Tuning from a higher tension to a lower tension can cause the strings to become loose and slip.

It is advisable to always tune from a lower to a higher tension, ensuring that the string is properly seated.

Regular maintenance is also key to maintaining tune. Cleaning the parts of dust and dirt, lubricating the gears of the mechanical tuners, and checking the fit of the wooden pegs helps keep the instrument in good condition.

Replacing worn parts with quality replacements can make a big difference in tuning stability.

Differences between classical and flamenco guitar

Although the causes of out of tune are similar in both types of guitar, there are some important differences.

Flamenco guitar strings are usually thinner and have lower tension, which may require more frequent adjustments. In addition, flamenco guitarists often use alternative tunings or capos (capotraste) to adapt to the tonality of the singer or the dance.

This makes it necessary to pay special attention to tuning before and during performance.

Conclusion

In short, a guitar goes out of tune due to a combination of mechanical, environmental, and maintenance factors. Changing strings regularly, keeping the tuners and bridge in good condition, and storing the guitar in a controlled environment are essential practices for maintaining good tuning.
