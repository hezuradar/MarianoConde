Maple wood for classical and flamenco guitar. What you should know.

Discover maple wood, a versatile wood for guitars, standing out on sides and backs for its bright and articulate sound.

Workshop Mariano Conde

Maple wood is one of the most versatile and appreciated materials in the construction of guitars, especially standing out in the manufacture of sides and back, and sometimes, necks.

Physical characteristics

Density and color

The maple has a white or pale yellow heartwood, while its sapwood is almost white with reddish tones. It is a semi-heavy wood, with an approximate density of 700 kg/m³ at 12% humidity. The young bark shows a distinctive shiny silver-gray color.

Structure

It has a generally straight fiber, occasionally wavy, with a grain that varies from very fine to fine. Its texture has a homogeneous structure, and the fine pores contribute to its high resistance.

Sound properties

Bright and articulate sound

Maple produces a bright and articulate sound, characterized by good attack and sustain. It accentuates treble and midrange with good volume, increasing the vividness of the sound that makes the solo stand out over the accompaniment.

Tonal characteristics

The density and rigidity of maple contribute to a flared and crystalline sound, providing great punch in the lower frequencies. It is especially effective in adding brightness and definition to the sound when combined with more neutral woods such as spruce.

Technical aspects

Main varieties

There are different types of maple for guitars:

  • Birds eye maple
  • Rock maple
  • Quilt maple (“curly”)
  • Flamed maple

Structural properties

  • High wear resistance
  • Moderate durability
  • Good dimensional stability
  • Susceptibility to fungal and insect attacks

Practical considerations

Workability

Maple wood is extremely difficult to carve due to its high strength, but allows for an excellent finish in terms of staining, varnishing and polishing. It is important to note that it can easily yellow during work.

Ideal applications

Maple is particularly suitable for:

  • Masts (Very occasionally)
  • Hoops and bottoms
  • Combination with neutral woods to improve tonal definition such as spruce and cedar
