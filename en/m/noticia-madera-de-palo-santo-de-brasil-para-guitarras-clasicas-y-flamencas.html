Brazilian Palo Santo wood for classical and flamenco guitars: What you should know

Discover all about Brazilian Rosewood (Dalbergia nigra), the historic choice of luthiers for high-end classical and flamenco guitars

Workshop Mariano Conde

Brazilian Rosewood, known worldwide as Rio Rosewood or Dalbergia nigra, is one of the most emblematic and coveted woods in the construction of classical and flamenco guitars. Its prestige among luthiers and musicians is due to a unique combination of physical, acoustic and aesthetic properties, which have made it the historical reference for high-end instruments.

Brazilian Rosewood

The Brazilian Palo Santo is native to the Atlantic Forest, a tropical coastal region of eastern Brazil, especially in the state of Bahia, which is why it is also known as Bahian Palo Santo. This wood has been used for centuries by the best guitar makers and continues to be a favorite for those seeking the highest level of quality and tradition.

Physical characteristics

Density and color

Brazilian Palo Santo is a very hard and heavy wood, with a density that can exceed 1,000 kg/m³. This condition changes once it is dried over time.
Its color varies from intense red to different shades of dark brown, sometimes reaching almost black. It is common to find black veins that form distinctive patterns, known as "spider webs" or "landscapes", which provide unmatched visual beauty.

Structure

It has a generally straight or slightly interlocking grain and a fine to medium texture. Its surface, once sanded and polished, offers a great natural shine. Although it is a dense and oily wood, which can make gluing difficult, its workability is appreciated by experienced luthiers.

Sound properties

Deep and crystal clear sound

Brazilian Rosewood is renowned for its extraordinary resonance and its ability to produce a deep, rich, crystalline tone. It offers exceptional tonal balance, with powerful bass, brilliant treble and a great richness of nuances. This sound character makes it the preferred option for concert guitars, both classical and flamenco, where projection, sustain and definition are sought.

tonal response

Its ability to enhance the nuances and expressiveness of the player has made legendary guitarists and renowned builders, such as Torres, Esteso and Santos, choose this wood for their most emblematic instruments.

Technical aspects

Drying

Drying Brazilian Palo Santo is a slow and careful process due to its high density and oil content. This treatment is essential to ensure the structural stability and durability of the instrument over the years.

Durability

It is a wood that is extremely fragile to breakage, the older and drier it is, the more complicated it is to work with and then preserve as an instrument since it resembles glass in terms of delicacy.

Practical considerations

Advantages

  • Deep, rich and crystalline sound, ideal for concert guitars.
  • Great structural stability and resistance to the passage of time.
  • Unmistakable aesthetics thanks to its unique veins and colors.

Ideal applications

  • Construction of backs and sides on high-end classical and flamenco guitars.
  • Instruments aimed at professional performers and collectors.

Sustainability and availability

Due to overexploitation and reduction of its natural habitat, the Brazilian Rosewood is listed on CITES Appendix I and the IUCN Red List as a vulnerable species. Its international trade is very restricted, which has increased its value and difficulty of access. For this reason, most of the current guitars made with this wood come from old stocks or from wood cut and dried decades ago.

In our guitar shop we have a stock of around 300 sets-units (sides and backs) with a natural drying time that ranges between 30-50 years.

Brazilian Palo Santo wood in classical and flamenco guitars

In summary, Brazilian Rosewood represents excellence in the construction of classical and flamenco guitars, combining majestic aesthetics, outstanding durability and unsurpassed acoustic properties as long as it is properly cared for. Its deep and nuanced sound, along with its natural beauty and stability, make it the favorite choice of luthiers and musicians looking for instruments of the highest quality and tradition.
