What are the parts or pieces of a classical guitar?

Discover the fundamental parts of a classical guitar: head, neck and soundboard. Know each element in this musical instrument

Workshop Mariano Conde

TheguitarClassical is a stringed musical instrument composed of three main sections: the head, the neck and the soundboard. Each of these parts fulfills a specific function in producing the characteristic sound of the instrument.

the head

The head or headstock is the upper part of the guitar where the headstock is located. This system is made up of six pegs that allow you to adjust the tension of the strings to tune the instrument. The shape and design of the head may vary depending on the manufacturer, being a distinctive element of each builder. In our case the already established and globally recognized “Media luna”.

Headstock and pegs

At the top of the guitar is the headstock, which houses six pegs arranged symmetrically, three on each side. This mechanism uses a worm screw system that allows you to adjust the tension of the strings for tuning.

nut

This small piece is generally made of cow bone and in the past, sometimes, of ivory. Its function is to separate and support the strings, maintaining the correct height in relation to the neck and is a fundamental part for the perfect tuning of the instrument.

The mast

Fingerboard

The fingerboard is an elongated and narrow piece that covers the neck, with approximate dimensions of 470x75x9 mm. This piece houses the frets and is where the fingers press the strings to produce the different notes. In our case, 100% of the time it is made inebony woodcoming from Africa.

The fingerboard covers the front part of the neck and is usually made of hard woods such as ebony, since it must resist the constant friction of the fingers and the strings and withstand the constant pressure exerted by the strings in constant tuning.

Frets

They are small, thin nickel silver bars inserted perpendicularly into the fingerboard that divide the neck into semitones. Pressing a string against a fret changes its effective length and, therefore, its tuning.

rope pull

String pull, also known as scale length, is the distance between the nut and the saddle. This measurement is crucial as it determines the tuning and tension of the strings. On our guitars, the measurements are 664 mm (long throw) and 650 mm (short throw).

The Resonance Box

Soundboard

The top is considered the most crucial component of the classical guitar, being largely responsible for the quality of the sound. Its approximate dimensions are 550x200x4 mm, and it is generally manufactured in two pieces. It is the front part of the sound box, generally made of wood such as spruce or cedar, each offering different acoustic characteristics. In its center is the mouth, surrounded by the decorative rosette.

Back and sides

The sides and back are fundamental elements that make up the soundboard of the guitar. The rings are the side walls that join the lid to the bottom, while the bottom is the back of the body. These components are made with select woods that directly influence the sound quality of the instrument. Learn about the most used woods here. The most used woods are cypress, Indian rosewood, Brazilian rosewood, maple, koa, Madagascar, Majagua, Dorado, etc., each providing unique tonal characteristics.

bridge

Located on the soundboard, the bridge transmits the vibrations of the strings to the soundboard, amplifying the sound. It includes the saddle, which keeps the strings separated and aligned, which, well placed and constructed, is a fundamental part of tuning.

Structural elements:

Mouth and rosette

The mouth is the circular opening in the soundboard that allows the projection of sound. It is decorated with the rosette, a characteristic ornamental element of classical guitars, built with mosaic, feather and “fileteria” techniques.

Zoque

It is the base where the soundboard is fixed and allows the union between the body of the guitar and the neck.

Hoops

It is the curved part located on the side of the guitar that rests on the guitarist's legs during performance.

Additional considerations

The set of all these parts forms a complex instrument where each element fulfills a specific function. The quality of the materials and the precision in the construction of each component are essential to achieve a quality instrument.

Thewood selectionfor each part it is crucial, since each type of wood provides specific characteristics to the final sound of the instrument. As guitar makers-luthiers we pay special attention to the selection of materials and the construction process, ensuring that each part fulfills its function optimally.

The combination and quality of all these elements determines both the sound and the durability of the instrument. Each part has been meticulously designed to contribute to the production of the characteristic sound of the Spanish classical guitar and specifically of our guitars that generation after generation have passed on all the knowledge, trial and error from parents to children until what is today the Mariano Conde guitar that we build and offer to our customers.

Maintenance and care

To preserve the quality of the guitar, it is essential to keep each of these parts in good condition. The varnish must be kept intact to protect the wood, the strings must be changed regularly, and the fingerboard must be cleaned and conditioned periodically but never with oils or miracle products that have become so fashionable these days, it is the only wood that is not varnished and the pore being open is not advisable to pour any type of liquid on it. Proper maintenance will ensure that the guitar maintains its sonic and structural qualities over time.

Understanding these parts is not only important for guitar makers, but also for musicians, as it allows for better care and use of the instrument.

Each component contributes uniquely to the characteristic sound of the classical guitar, creating that distinctive tone that has made it one of the most versatile and appreciated instruments in music.
