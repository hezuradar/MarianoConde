Guitar manufacturing process Mariano Conde

Discover the artisanal process of manufacturing Mariano Conde guitars, heirs of a centuries-old tradition. Seven key stages to creating unique, high-quality instruments.

Workshop Mariano Conde

Mariano Conde guitars represent a craft tradition that dates back to 1915, keeping alive the legacy of the old house Conde Hermanos nephews successors of Domingo Esteso

The artisanal manufacture of Mariano Conde guitars follows a meticulous process of seven fundamental stages that last between 3-5 months of waiting-construction and can reach 1 year or more depending on the volume of work and orders they have at the time of the order, preserving a tradition that goes back more than a century.

First: wood selection

The construction begins with a rigorous selection of noble woods that have been subjected to a natural drying process for 15 to 90 years, even reaching 100 years. They are carefully chosen:

  • Lid: German spruce pine or Canadian red cedar
  • Back and sides: Sycamore, Mahogany, Indian Palo Santo, Cypress, Palo Santo, Madagascar, Maple, Cocobolo, Brazilian Palo Santo, Golden, Koa, Mahogany, Ebony or Maple
  • Fingerboard: Ebony

Each wood is selected for its specific acoustic properties: hardness, flexibility, drying and elasticity.

Second: manufacturing parts

All the pieces that will make up the guitar are meticulously made:

  • Neck and head size
  • Cutting and calibrating the cover, back and sides
  • Bending of the hoops
  • Preparation of bar system, internal bracing and reinforcements
  • Manufacturing of the bridge

Third: gluing and assembly

This crucial phase requires absolute precision:

  • Union of the rings to the bottom or lid
  • Placing the bracing system on the lid
  • Assembling the neck with the soundboard
  • Gluing borders
  • Gluing the fingerboard
  • Bridge installation
  • Natural glues and traditional assembly techniques are used.

Fourth: sanding and finishing

The sanding process includes:

  • Progressive sanding with different grains
  • Polishing of all surfaces
  • Joint Refinement
  • Profile finishing

This step is essential to achieve a perfectly smooth surface.

Fifth: varnishing

A traditional finish is applied that includes:

  • Wood sealing
  • Multiple coats of shellac or nitro
  • Intermediate polishing between layers
  • Final finishing with a wrist

Varnishing enhances the natural beauty of the wood and protects it from external agents such as humidity, sweat and scratches. It's the guitar suit. A dress comparable to a "Balenciaga".

Sixth: set-up

Fine adjustments are made:

  • Carving of the nuts (head and bridge) in cow bone
  • Fret plane and rounding of the same.
  • Sanding and polishing of frets and ebony
  • Gluing the pickguard if the guitar needs it (Flamenco only)
  • String height adjustment
  • High Precision Headstock Installation
  • Action Calibration
  • Preliminary tuning and acoustic tests

Seventh: delivery to customer

The process ends with:

  • Final quality control
  • Final tuning
  • Instrument documentation
  • Certificate of authenticity
  • Personalized delivery

Details of the artisanal construction of guitars Mariano Conde

Limited production of approximately 30-50 guitars per year ensures attention to detail and top quality in each instrument. Each guitar is fire-engraved with the initials “MC” and a handwritten interior label showing the model with its serial number and the year of construction, thus certifying its authenticity as a true masterpiece of Spanish lutherie.

The resulting sound quality is characterized by its depth and projection, with intense and lively notes, especially suitable for flamenco and classical music.
