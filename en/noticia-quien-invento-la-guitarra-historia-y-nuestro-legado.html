Who invented the guitar: history and our legacy

Discover the living history of the Spanish guitar through four generations of Mariano Conde luthiers, guardians of a centuries-old tradition.

Workshop Mariano Conde

Us,Mariano Conde father and son, we want to share with you a story that is not only personal, but part of the very heart of Spanish culture:the history of the guitar. Asking who invented the guitar is, for us, more than a historical fact; It is an invitation to understand the long path of tradition, passion and craft that we have had the privilege of guarding and continuing.

The origin of a shared dream

For more than a century, our family has been fortunate to be part of the evolution and perfection of the Spanish guitar. The truth is that no one can take sole credit for the invention of the guitar. This instrument is the result of the collective work of many generations, cultures and artisans who, over time, have been carving, tuning and making this voice of wood and strings vibrate.

The guitar we build today has its roots in what were once rudimentary string instruments—thevihuelas— and other forms that coexisted in the Iberian Peninsula, shaped by Arab and European influence. The Master's LegacyAntonio de TorresIn the 19th century it marked us deeply, setting standards and forms that we continue to defend with pride.

The magic that unites us with the instrument

We can say that the guitar speaks because we listen to it, because we know every millimeter of its construction. The mouth, that magical opening in the center of the soundboard, is our meeting point with the instrument: the breath between the wood and the air. For us, this detail is fundamental: it must be perfect, neither too big nor too small, because in that measurement lies the unique voice of each guitar.

Every day we work with wood as with an old friend, recognizing its character and adapting to it. We choose the noblest woods, we select the varnish that best protects without impeding your voice and we take care of every detail, from the rosette that adorns the soundhole to the internal braces that give life to the instrument.

The guitar as a bridge between generations

But above all, we believe that the guitar is abridge. It unites us, those who played before and those who will play after. When we see the great flamenco and classical masters take up our guitars, we know that they are not only playing wood and strings, but centuries of culture, passion and craft.

We, father and son, are part of this chain that is not broken by time or change. For this reason, each guitar we build carries our footprint and our soul, because we know that when it plays, it also speaks for us and for an entire story that we want to take care of and continue telling.

Conclusion: a living legacy

In the end, the guitar does not have a single inventor: all of us who have loved it and worked with passion have invented and reinvented it. From the first generation, our great uncle and founderDomingo Esteso, even your most gifted studentMariano Conde Salamanca (Mariano I), and we,Mariano Conde Cavia (Marian II)andMariano Conde Navarro (Mariano III)Four generations since 1915 we continue to work with humility and pride, keeping alive a tradition that is more alive than ever, capturing in each instrument the soul that is born from the wood and the emotion that only the guitar can transmit.

This is our legacy and our essence. With every guitar we build and deliver, we reaffirm thatThe history of the guitar is also the history of our family: a legacy that we continue to cultivate together, in every note that sounds and in every soul that vibrates with it.

Mariano Conde
